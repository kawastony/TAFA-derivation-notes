# How to tell B1 from B2

Distance-modulus difference vs B1, Δμ = 5 log₁₀(d_L^{B2}/d_L^{B1}), freeze today, α=1.

| z | Δμ f=10 | Δμ f=30 | Δμ f=100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3 | −0.0005 | −0.0001 | ~0 |
| 1.0 | −0.011 | −0.001 | ~0 |
| 2.0 | −0.047 | −0.006 | ~0 |

Current SN Hubble-diagram scatter is ~0.1 mag. Stacked, ~0.02 mag shifts can show. So:

- **f=10** is in principle visible at z≳1, and already ugly in w(1)=−0.61. Treat as excluded if you want DE-like w.
- **f=30** is *not* separable from B1 with present SN. BAO E(z) at z=1 differs by ~0.4%. Need a dedicated E(z) program, not a slogan.
- **f=100** is B1 for data.

Keeping both therefore means: B1, and B2-at-f≳30 as a near-copy of B1 plus a thawing tail you cannot yet see. It does *not* mean two observationally different dark energies today.

The Δy → z∼0.3 tick is at a redshift where Δμ is millimag. It does not pick the room.
